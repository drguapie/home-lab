## Why I Built This Lab

I wanted a dedicated environment where I could safely learn Linux, networking, cybersecurity, and system administration without risking my primary computer. Rather than purchasing expensive enterprise equipment, I focused on affordable hardware that provides practical, hands-on experience while remaining within my budget.

---

## Lessons Learned

One of the biggest lessons I've learned so far is to verify existing infrastructure before purchasing additional hardware.

While expanding my home network, I initially purchased MoCA adapters to extend Ethernet over coaxial cable. 
After troubleshooting the issue, I discovered that the upstairs coaxial outlet simply needed to be activated by my Internet service provider. 
Although the adapters were ultimately unnecessary, the experience reinforced the importance of diagnosing the problem first before investing in additional equipment.

## Reflection

I purchased the Raspberry Pi 5 starter kit in 2024 when I was first becoming interested in Linux and home labs. At the time, I didn't have a clear plan for how I would use it, but I knew it was a popular platform for learning.

Looking back, I probably spent more than I needed to for my goals. The complete starter kit was convenient, but I later discovered that a refurbished desktop like my Dell OptiPlex provided significantly more performance for Linux development at a lower cost.

Even so, I don't consider the purchase a waste. It introduced me to Linux hardware, and I plan to repurpose the Raspberry Pi as a dedicated server for services such as Pi-hole, Docker, or network monitoring as my home lab continues to grow.


## Lessons Learned

- Research hardware based on project requirements rather than popularity.
- Refurbished business desktops can provide excellent value for Linux labs.
- Verify existing network infrastructure before purchasing additional networking hardware.
- Build the lab incrementally instead of purchasing everything at once.
